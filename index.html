Overview

UTV limit straps (also called bump straps or droop straps) limit how far the suspension droops when a wheel leaves the ground. Without them, the suspension drops to full extension on every big air landing or off-camber section, which over-extends CV axles, shocks, and brake lines. The failure modes are expensive: bent CV shafts, blown shock seals, and torn brake lines. Quality limit straps prevent all of these failure modes for a fraction of the repair cost. They're not glamorous — nobody takes photos of their limit straps — but experienced UTV riders consider them mandatory maintenance on any machine used in serious terrain. The adjustment range matters: too short and you limit suspension travel; too long and you still get over-extension. The sweet spot depends on your machine and riding style.

Buying Guide — What to Know Before You Buy

What Do Limit Straps Actually Do?

Limit straps connect the chassis to the A-arm or suspension arm, limiting how far the suspension droops when unloaded. They prevent CV axle over-extension, which causes bent shafts and torn boots — the most common expensive UTV suspension failure.

Correct Adjustment Length

Too short: limits suspension travel and makes the ride harsh. Too long: still allows damaging over-extension. Correct adjustment allows 90% of rated droop without full extension. Set with the suspension at rest, not under load.

Webbing Material Quality

2-inch rated nylon webbing handles most UTV suspension loads. Aircraft-grade hardware (buckles, hooks, loops) resists corrosion and fatigue. Avoid limit straps with plastic hardware — they crack under repeated load cycles.

Installation Frequency

Inspect limit straps at every oil change. Look for fraying, cracking, or UV degradation. Replace immediately if any damage is visible — a failed strap offers no protection. Budget straps may need annual replacement under heavy use.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do all UTVs need limit straps?

Any UTV used in terrain with significant elevation changes, jumps, or off-camber sections benefits from limit straps. Machines used primarily on groomed trails or flat terrain can go without, but the cost of straps vs. CV repair makes them worth it for almost anyone.

What's the difference between limit straps and bump stops?

Bump stops limit suspension compression (upward travel). Limit straps limit suspension droop (downward travel). Most UTVs need both for complete suspension protection.

How do I measure the correct strap length?

Measure the distance between mounting points at full droop, then set the strap 10–15% shorter than that measurement. This prevents full droop while allowing near-full suspension travel for comfort.

Can limit straps be used with aftermarket suspension?

Yes. Aftermarket suspension lifts and extended-travel components often require longer limit straps. Re-measure and set straps after any suspension modification.

What happens if a limit strap fails on the trail?

The suspension will over-extend, potentially bending CV axles or damaging shock shafts. It's a trailside repair situation — bring a spare set if you're doing serious terrain far from the trailhead.

Can I install limit straps myself?

Yes. Installation is straightforward — locate the mounting points on your machine's chassis and A-arms, route the strap between them, and adjust to the correct length. The most difficult part is finding the correct mounting holes, which vary by machine.

Do limit straps affect ride quality?

When properly adjusted, limit straps are not felt during normal riding. You only feel them at the extreme end of droop travel. Incorrectly shortened straps will make the ride harsh over whoops and terrain variations.

How often should limit straps be replaced?

Inspect at every oil change. Nylon webbing degrades in UV exposure — straps used outdoors in sunny climates may need replacement annually. Premium straps with UV-stabilized webbing last longer.
